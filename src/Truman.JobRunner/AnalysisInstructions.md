Analyse the article below. Report its argument, not its wording.

Produce:

- tags: up to 10 tags or keywords that capture the essence of the article. Prefer tags that
  would group this article with others on the same subject, over tags that describe only this
  one article.
- claim: the one thing the article asks the reader to accept or take away, in a single plain
  sentence. State it outright, including any answer the headline withholds. If the article is a
  bare report of an event, the claim is what happened. If it is a roundup or digest of unrelated
  items with no single argument, say so.
- support: what the claim rests on, most load-bearing first - evidence, findings, named
  sources, reasoning. At most five, one short phrase each. Only what is actually in the article.
  For a roundup, the support is the items themselves: give one entry per item, saying what that
  item is, rather than describing the list. List every item, however many there are.
- padding: material the claim does not rest on - scene-setting, anecdote used only as a hook,
  restatement, biography, unrelated digression. At most five, one short phrase each. Empty if
  there is none.
- headline_gap: how the article's own title relates to the claim. "none" if it states the
  claim, "overstates" if it promises more than the article delivers, "withholds" if it
  deliberately hides the answer to make the reader click.
- source_depth: "full" if the text below is the article body, "summary" if it is only a
  headline and a line or two of blurb.
